# Kinpaw landing page: what it does today and how to make it feel alive

I read every component on the page and looked at it in headless Chrome at desktop (1440px) and phone (390px) widths. The Chrome extension wasn't connected, so these are static screenshots. The dev server is still running at `localhost:3000`.

## What you have

**The story.** The page is "Seven days with Mochi". The full direction is in `apps/web/.impeccable/surfaces/` and `docs/memory/website-seven-days.md`.

| Section | What happens |
|---|---|
| Day 1 (hero) | The sky matches the visitor's own hour, set by an inline script before the page paints. The headline words rise in. Mochi has working needs meters and Feed, Play and Pet buttons, using the app's real rules from `@app/contract`. |
| Day 2, morning | Eight real voice clips. |
| Day 3, night | A scripted replay of a conversation where Mochi keeps one fact. |
| Days 4 and 6 | One line each on plain ground. |
| Day 5, afternoon | A lock screen showing one notification from Mochi. |
| Day 7, dusk | "I missed you! How did the exam go?" |
| After the week | Pet builder, the real app (video and a row of phone screens), pricing, FAQ, then a closing night where the pet you built is asleep. |
| Throughout | A fixed seven-day strip along the bottom follows your scroll. |

**How the motion works.** Almost all of it is CSS in `globals.css`:
- **One-time entrances:** the hero headline's word rise, `.enter`, and `<Reveal>`. `<Reveal>` fades and lifts an element the first time it scrolls into view; there are 25 of them on the page.
- **Small loops:** the pet breathing, stars twinkling, sound bars, speech bubbles popping in, and the bursts after a care tap.
- **Skies:** each day is its own `<Scene>` section with a fixed gradient built in `lib/sky.ts`.
- **The `motion` library:** it's installed, and `MotionProvider` wraps the whole page. Its only use is `Magnetic`, which sits inside `StoreBadges`. Those badges only render once the app is live (`IS_LIVE`), so today the page ships the library and uses none of it.

## Why it reads as static

1. **Every element enters the same way.** All 25 reveals are the same fade-up, played once. After that, nothing on the page responds to scrolling.
2. **The week jumps between days instead of passing.** The idea is time going by, but each day is a separate band with a fixed sky. The changes are hard seams: you can see day 2 fade to white and then cut to night, and day 3 cut from dark to blue. The sun and moon are fixed dots, and the clouds don't move.
3. **There are five separate Mochis, all still drawings.** `PetSvg` says it is "a still drawing" by design. The pet never blinks, never looks at you and never makes an entrance. When its mood changes on day 1, the face just switches.
4. **The world ends after day 7.** The builder, app, pricing and FAQ sections are flat navy, and on desktop there's a large empty band between the builder and "And this is the app itself". The sky only comes back for the closing.
5. **Days 4 and 6 are the dullest part of the page.** They could easily be the most cinematic.
6. **A small one:** on a night visit, the hero moon sits right against the period of "today." Worth checking whether that's intended.

## Ideas

### Three big ideas

**A. Scrolling moves time.** Put one sky behind the whole week and drive it with scroll progress:
- The sun arcs across each day and sets, the stars fade in and the moon rises.
- Days 4 and 6 become time-lapses: about one screen of scrolling fast-forwards a whole day while Mochi sleeps and its needs drain gently, under the existing line "Nothing happens to Mochi".
- To build it, use CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline`, with `@property` so gradient colours can animate), and fall back to `motion`'s `useScroll` where those aren't supported. The colours still come from `art.sky`.

**B. Mochi feels alive.**
- It blinks.
- Its eyes follow the cursor. `@app/brand` already has the eye positions (`EYE_X`, `EYE_Y`).
- On load it drops onto its stand with a little squash.
- You can tap the pet itself to pet it, with hearts at the pointer.
- Moods crossfade instead of switching.
- If you're idle for about 10 seconds, it says something.
- Optional: a tiny Mochi rides in the week strip and its mood changes each day.

**C. You can watch a memory travel.** On day 3, the fact "Has an exam Friday" pops out of the reply as a small tag and flies into the week strip. It rides along through days 4 to 6, then on day 7 drops out and settles under "How did the exam go?". The page's whole claim, that Mochi remembers, becomes something you can see. Because the strip is fixed on screen, the tag can animate in and out of it with `motion`'s layout animations.

### Upgrades for each scene

- **Day 1:** the needs meters fill up on load, the speech bubble types itself out, and the care buttons have a springy press.
- **Day 2:** the sound bars move with the clip's real volume (Web Audio `AnalyserNode`) instead of a fake loop. Switching between cat and dog animates instead of swapping.
- **Day 5:** the notification springs down from the top with a small phone-buzz wiggle. As the scene scrolls in, the clock rolls forward from 9:00 to 2:10, so time passes here too.
- **Day 7:** the line arrives word by word in time with the voice, and a warm lamp glow spreads behind the bubble.
- **Week strip:** a progress fill between days, a label that slides smoothly to the active day, and day numbers that roll as they change.

### After the week

- **Keep the world going:** the post-week sections become "the evening after", with a moon drifting slowly, instead of dropping to flat navy. Fill the empty band between the builder and the app section.
- **Builder:** each option change makes the pet pop with a squash. The dice button flicks through a few random looks before it lands. The name you type appears in a bubble over the pet.
- **Closing:** your pet curls up and the light dims as you scroll toward the footer.
- **App row:** drag it with momentum, and add some depth (tilt or parallax) to the phones as you scroll.

### Quick wins across the page

- Use the hero's word-rise animation on every section headline (today it's hero-only). This is cheap and you'd see it immediately.
- Give stars, clouds and the sun or moon slight parallax at different scroll speeds, and let the clouds drift slowly.
- Animate navigation from the landing page to `/whats-new` and `/pricing` with page transitions. I'd need to check Next 15.5's experimental flag for React's `ViewTransition` first.

## Guardrails from the page's brief

- **No new libraries.** The brief rules out GSAP or Lenis, but `motion` plus CSS covers everything above.
- **Brand colours only, and sound only after a tap.**
- **Reduced motion:** every new effect needs a still version. The global reduced-motion block covers the CSS animations, and `MotionConfig` covers `motion`, but anything driven by scroll or `requestAnimationFrame` has to check the setting itself.
- **Performance:**
  - The hero text must keep painting immediately; don't let it wait on an animation.
  - Most visitors are on a phone, so animate only transform and opacity.
- **New interactions need their analytics events registered first.** Tapping the pet, the dice roll and the travelling memory tag all go through the `events` skill before any code, and `WEEK_EVENTS` has to stay in step.

**My recommendation:** start with **A and B plus the quick wins**. Together they change how the whole page feels. Then do **C**, which is the most memorable story moment but needs A's sky in place to land.

Which direction appeals most? Once you pick, I can turn it into a spec in `docs/application/features/` with the `spec` skill, or build a prototype of one scene first so you can feel it before committing.
