## How the pet works today

- **Drawing:** every pet is drawn in code, with no image files. `packages/brand/src/pet.ts` turns an appearance into simple shapes (circles, ovals, paths), and `PetArt` draws them as flat SVG. The appearance is the species plus nine options (fur, pattern, marking colour, paws & chest and its colour, eyes, ears, body, tail), which gives about 36 million combinations per species. The website draws the same shapes.
- **Animation:** `PetStage` moves the whole pet as one stiff piece: it breathes, hops, leans, squashes and does a purr shiver. Only the eyes move on their own (blinks and glances).
- **Voice:** the pet speaks, but its mouth never moves.
- **Pet from a photo:** an AI model looks at the photo and picks the nearest option from each of our fixed lists.

## Why it looks like a 2D sticker

1. **Nothing moves on its own except the eyes.** The tail, ears, head, mouth and paws are fixed. Small movements like a tail swish or an ear flick are most of what makes an animal feel alive.
2. **It's flat.** Each part is one colour, with no light, roundness or fur.
3. **It has one pose.** It always faces forward and stands. It can't sit, turn its head or curl up asleep.
4. **It talks with its mouth shut.** In a talking-pet app, this is the most visible gap.
5. **Every pet has the same head.** There's no snout length or fur length, which is what makes a breed, or someone's own pet, recognisable.

## What the market shows

- **My Talking Tom** is built in Unity with almost 1,000 animations. What makes it work isn't realism: every place you touch (head, belly, tail, feet) gets its own reaction.
- **Duolingo** is the closest match to Kinpaw: flat 2D characters, made in a tool called Rive, whose mouths move in time with their speech.
- **Tolan**, a voice-first AI companion with stylised 3D characters, sells to young adults, our audience.

None of the successful apps uses realistic animals. A realistic animal that talks tends to look creepy, and it would clash with Kinpaw's cozy feel. I'd aim for **alive and soft**: stylised, with some roundness and light, reacting to touch, mouth moving as it speaks.

## The options, cheapest first

## Skia in plain terms

**What it is:** Skia is the drawing engine behind Chrome, Android and Flutter, made available in React Native. You draw with components that look like SVG's (`<Circle>`, `<Path>`), but it draws on the GPU. Two things make it worth it for us:
- **Shaders:** small GPU programs that colour each pixel. That's how a flat circle gets light, roundness and fur.
- **Smoother animation:** animations run without re-rendering React, which matters with six parts moving at once.

Moving our drawing to Skia is mostly mechanical, because the shapes are already plain data in `@app/brand`. The shaders are the new part; I'd write and explain them, and you'd judge the result by eye.

**Edge cases that matter most (the full table of 15 is in the research, section 10):**
- **Native rebuild:** it's native code, so it needs one rebuild, which can undo the Xcode signing fix. After that, drawing and shader changes are plain JavaScript and could ship as over-the-air updates.
- **One rich pet per screen:** each Skia canvas uses GPU memory. The builder grid and friends list stay SVG; only the main pet uses Skia.
- **Broken shaders:** a mistake in a shader draws nothing rather than crashing. Every shader gets a test, and a failure falls back to the SVG pet, so the stage is never blank.
- **Speed testing:** the Simulator and dev builds don't show real speed. Speed is checked on your iPhone with a release build.
- **App size:** about 6 MB more on iOS.
- **The website keeps SVG:** it gets the new motion but not the light and fur.

**Cost estimate:**
- Part A (animating today's SVG pet): 7–10 days.
- Part B (Skia 2.5D): 9–13 days.
- Total: about 4 weeks, with your checks between phases.
